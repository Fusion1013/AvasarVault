Floaters grow in swamps with high concentrations of natural gasses below the ground, close to the surface. They channel gas through long, spindly roots that anchor the balloon-like body to shallow ponds. The main body of the plant is a thick membrane, covered in warts and short hairs of roots. The inside of the plant is hollow, filled with natural gas. The plant will continue to expand during its lifetime, and once they reach a certain size the roots will detach from the ground, allowing the plant to lazily rise into the sky. At a certain height the body of the plant will burst, releasing spores that cover the inside.

Hovering slightly above the waters surface protects the body of the plant from surface-dwelling critters and smaller animals, though at the same time exposing the roots. The plant can sometimes be found floating in the waters, where the roots have been eaten away at by insects causing the plant to detach prematurely.

The bulb itself usually grows to around 1 meter in diameter before it is large enough to detach from the ground, though specimens as large as 2-3 meters in diameter have been found. The height they can reach before detaching varies greatly, with some hovering just above the surface, while others can reach 5-6 meters in height.
